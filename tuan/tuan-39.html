LOTUS COMPANY — MEMORANDUM

To:      All staff who answer the phone
From:    Mr. Kim
Subject: What to say when you do not know

Ms. Linh has kept a note of every call to the front desk for four weeks.
Two hundred and ten calls came in. In sixty-one of them the caller asked
something the front desk could not answer, and that is the part of the note
I want everyone to read.

Sixty-one is not a bad number. A front desk cannot hold the answer to
everything. What the note does show is that we handle those sixty-one
calls in three different ways, and only one of the three is any good.

The first way is to guess. Two callers were given a delivery date that
nobody had agreed, and both called back to complain when the date passed.
Never give a date that is not written down somewhere.

The second way is to say "I don't know" and stop there. That is honest, but
the caller puts the phone down with nothing at all, and half of them simply
call again an hour later.

The third way is the one I want. Say that you do not have the answer, and
then do one more thing: give the caller a name, a time, or a place. "Ms. Ha
holds that number — may I ask her to call you back this afternoon?" takes
four seconds longer than "I don't know", and it ends the call.

Ms. Linh will put a short list by the phone: who holds which figure. Ms.
Lan will keep it up to date. If your name is on that list and you move a
file, tell Ms. Lan the same day.

One last thing. Nobody here has ever been in the wrong for saying they did
not know. Two people have been in the wrong for guessing.

Mr. Kim
